# Product direction

## North star

**Open your science-fiction world, write 200 words today, and keep every fact about that world close at hand.**

200 Crappy Words is a local-first desktop writing environment for people building science-fiction novels and the worlds behind them. It should help a writer return every day, lower the emotional cost of beginning, and keep lore useful while prose is being written.

The app should not try to become a generic office suite, a competitive social network, or an encyclopedia that makes preparation feel more important than writing.

## Primary user

A solo fiction writer who:

- wants a gentle daily writing habit;
- is working on a novel, series, or interconnected setting;
- needs to track characters, places, factions, technology, and chronology;
- values ownership and portability of their creative work;
- may not want their unpublished manuscript sent to a cloud service; and
- prefers a focused tool over assembling many unrelated applications.

## Core loop

1. Open a world project.
2. See today's progress without ceremony or guilt.
3. Continue a scene or capture a new idea.
4. Refer to or create connected lore without losing the writing context.
5. Reach 200 words and receive a quiet moment of completion.
6. Leave knowing the work is safely saved and easy to resume.

## Product pillars

### Trustworthy writing

The app protects unsaved work, recovers from interruption, makes save state understandable, and keeps creative files portable.

### A humane daily practice

Two hundred words is the opinionated default. Progress, history, and optional streaks encourage return without punishing missed days.

### Worldbuilding in context

Characters, places, technology, factions, species, events, and research are linked to the manuscript and available beside it.

### Long-form structure

Scenes and chapters can be outlined, reordered, revised, and exported without forcing the writer into a single planning method.

### Science-fiction continuity

Dates, travel, character ages, inventions, factions, and canon status can be checked across a complex setting.

## Non-goals for the early product

- Real-time collaboration
- Social publishing or community features
- Required accounts or cloud storage
- Rich page-layout and typesetting
- Replacing a final copy-editing or publishing suite
- Generative AI as a requirement for the core workflow
- Building every possible worldbuilding visualization before the writing loop is excellent

## Experience principles

- The empty state should always offer an obvious way to begin.
- Common writing actions should be keyboard accessible.
- Advanced structure should appear progressively rather than crowding the editor.
- Metrics should inform and encourage, never judge.
- A writer should be able to inspect and back up their project without this app.
- Product language should feel warm, concise, and slightly playful without becoming cute at the expense of trust.

## Success signals

The product is succeeding when a writer can:

- trust it with an unfinished draft;
- understand today's progress at a glance;
- find any established fact quickly while drafting;
- reorganize a long manuscript without losing material;
- catch meaningful continuity mistakes; and
- leave and return without reconstructing their mental context.
